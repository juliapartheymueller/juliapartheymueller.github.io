Data Science @ Uni Vienna

Julia Partheymüller is a Senior Scientist at the Department of Government at the University of Vienna. She is a member of the Vienna Center for Electoral Research (VieCER), where she coordinates the survey data collection of the Austrian National Election Study (AUTNES). She is also a member of the Research Network Data Science at the University of Vienna.

Her research focuses on comparative politics, democracy, and elections, with a focus on Austria, Germany, and comparative European contexts. She studies how citizens form political judgements, participate in elections, and evaluate democratic institutions and government performance. Her work addresses electoral integrity, satisfaction with democracy, and the dynamics of public opinion, linking citizens’ attitudes and behaviour to broader questions of democratic representation, accountability, and legitimacy.

Another strand of her research examines political communication and the relationship between information, misperceptions, and political behaviour. She investigates how traditional and digital media shape citizens’ beliefs and attitudes, as well as how perceived threats and psychological biases affect opinion formation. This includes research on how citizens understand the European Union and how misperceptions shape their attitudes towards European integration.

Further interdisciplinary work addresses the interface between science, society, and policymaking, particularly in the fields of public health and crisis management. She examines how citizens perceive and respond to policy design and communication, with particular attention to the role of trust in expertise and government. Her research contributes behavioural insights to inform policy and organisational design, helping institutions respond to citizens’ concerns and support informed public debate.

Methodologically, she specialises in survey research and quantitative methods, with extensive experience in large-scale data collection, survey design, survey experiments, and longitudinal analysis. She also contributes to the development of research data and digital resources for the wider scientific community.

She previously worked as a DAAD Lecturer at the University of Essex and was part of the German Longitudinal Election Study (GLES) team at the Mannheim Centre for European Social Research (MZES). She holds a doctorate in the Social Sciences from the University of Mannheim and studied Political Science at Free University Berlin and the University of Hamburg.
